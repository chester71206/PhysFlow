# PAC-NeRF WonderPlay reverse AD: nine-scene result (2026-11-11)

All nine scenes have at least 50 optimizer updates using rasterized WonderPlay pixel-flow loss against fixed observed RAFT flow and Warp reverse AD. Each run requested `--max-points 20000` for the flow objective; this is not an MPM particle cap. Letter's existing 74-update run was retained. Every evaluation JSON matches the SHA-256 of that scene's final `ckpt/sim.json`. Epochs are contiguous with no gaps or duplicates. Of 474 recorded updates, 471 lower the loss when immediately re-forwarded; Cat's epochs 10–12 have small transient increases, while its final loss is below its initial loss. A descending update does not establish full renderer gradient correctness.

`E`, `ν`, `Y`, `η`, and `φ` denote Young's modulus, Poisson ratio, yield stress, plastic viscosity, and friction angle. Initial/final values below are solver parameters. EPE is in pixels; PSNR is dB. Material RE is a mean relative error against the PAC-NeRF GT parameters after the conversions in `scripts/score_universal_pacnerf_re.py`. Cream and Toothpaste report a **partial** mean over μ, κ, and yield stress because the canonical scorer does not yet certify viscosity's GT-unit conversion.

## Gradient limitations

The initial full-loss gates pass for Letter, Torus, Bird, Cat, Cream, and Trophy. Droplet initially used a stagewise MPM check with same-sign full derivatives, then passed the full-loss check at its final checkpoint. Playdoh and Toothpaste used stagewise MPM checks with same-sign full derivatives. Their optimizer steps are reverse AD, not finite-difference optimization.

For Toothpaste, fixed-direction pair tests show stable AD/FD agreement for target-position projected displacement (pair 8: AD 1794.16, FD 1794.1–1795.3; pair 9: AD 1823.88, FD 1816.7–1826.3). Source-position FD over the same direction changes sharply with renderer-only perturbation size (pair 9: +52.5 to −2766.6, versus AD −907.5). Playdoh shows the same pattern: pair 9 target AD 59.10 and FD 58.75–59.32, but source AD 0.281 and FD 2.10–26.02. These tests isolate non-smoothness or numerical discontinuity in the source rasterization/visibility path at the full sample count; they do not rule out an additional backward implementation defect. Neither scene is marked as having passed a full renderer gradient FD test.

The canonical scorer leaves Cream/Toothpaste viscosity RE unspecified. From Warp's `SCALE_PV=1e4` and the same spatial stress conversion used for μ and yield, one can **infer** `η_physical = 1e4·η_solver/scale_origin²`. That inference gives full mean RE 98.61% for Cream and 99.90% for Toothpaste; it is stored separately in the JSON and is not treated as a certified GT-unit conversion.
