# Droplet PLY as independent geometry supervision

## Provenance and contents

The official [PAC-NeRF README](https://github.com/xuan-li/PAC-NeRF#optional-groundtruth-particle-data) offers `simulation_data.zip` as an optional ground-truth particle sequence. It separately describes PLY files written by its own Taichi MPM inference under `checkpoint/<scene>/simulation`. The local `model/pacnerf/droplet/pcd/{0..13}.ply` files have not been matched by checksum to the optional archive, so their *exact* provenance is not independently proven here. The local PhysFlow original trainer treats them as target geometry using framewise Chamfer distance, not indexed particle distance (`simulation_memory_safe.py`, around lines 638–643).

Each local PLY is binary little-endian with 324,080 vertices and exactly three `double` properties: `x`, `y`, `z`. There is no particle ID, color, velocity, mass or timestamp attribute. The Gaussian checkpoint is a different point set (125,344 Gaussian vertices in its PLY header); indexed PLY-to-Gaussian correspondence is not available.

Read-only coordinate inspection: frame 0 has bounds approximately x=[-0.2012,0.2014], y=[0.1868,0.8079], z=[-0.2015,0.2016]. Frame 1 has exactly the same horizontal bounds and y bounds shifted down by 0.029383. For a fixed random sample of 5,000 indices, the 10th/50th/90th percentile same-index displacement is 0.029383/0.029383/0.029383 for 0→1, and 0.046397/0.046397/0.046397 for 1→2. A nearest-neighbor match has much smaller distance because neighboring particles are spatially dense; therefore nearest-neighbor distance is **not** a suitable test of index identity. A random permutation gives median 0→1 distance 0.223. The spatially coherent, equal displacement at fixed indices across two consecutive frames is strong evidence that the PLY sequence retains particle order, although the files do not explicitly certify IDs. By frame 13 the same-index median displacement from frame 0 is 0.383, consistent with evolving deformation; this alone neither proves nor disproves long-range identity.

## Coordinate, timing and material comparison

The local alignment manifest maps image `r_0_i` to `pcd/i.ply`, source time `i/24` seconds, and Warp MPM target step rounded to `substep_dt=0.0002` seconds. The first selected target time is 0; for frame 1 the Warp time is 0.0416 versus the source 0.0416667 seconds. The official Droplet config uses `dt=1/4800` and a viscous-fluid Taichi model. These are close but different numerical time grids; the manifest is the correct place to validate every frame. Warp's scene loads Gaussian positions into MPM after rotation, scaling and translation, then its original geometry loss applies the inverse transforms before comparing to PLY in world coordinates. Thus a **world-coordinate framewise Chamfer** comparison is already implemented, but an initial-frame bounds/overlay audit is still necessary before interpreting small loss differences as material effects. The source particles (324,080) and Gaussian simulator particles have different geometry and counts; direct indexed position MSE is inappropriate.

Official PAC-NeRF Droplet uses `MPMSimulator.viscous_fluid`, `global_mu`, `global_kappa`; its fluid stress contains `kappa*(J-J^-6)*I + mu*sym(C)*J`, with `kappa=lambda+2mu/3`. The official model also scales particle coefficients by `alpha^3`. Our Warp Droplet uses `newtonian_model=strain_rate`: the rate-pressure stress has the same broad form, but its J comes from the determinant of a full deformation gradient and it clamps the volume to >=0.05. Its `mu` and `lambda` are computed from internal E/nu with an explicit `1e7` factor, and physical scoring additionally divides by `scale_origin^2`. Algebraically, `mu=E_phys/[2(1+nu)]` and `kappa=E_phys/[3(1-2nu)]` permit a same-model parameter conversion, but **this is not proof of dynamical equivalence** between the original Taichi GT simulation and our Warp scene. Initial Warp E=3e-7, nu=0.4995 correspond to physical mu≈0.376, kappa≈375.4 under the current scorer; table GT is mu=200, kappa=100000. The derived solver GT E≈1.597e-4 and nu≈0.499001 depend on that scorer and scale.

## What can be tested now

* **Independent 3D supervision:** the PLY positions can serve as framewise target geometry through the existing Chamfer definition, subject to confirming that these local files came from the optional official GT archive. The videos and masks are independent observed images for RAFT and PSNR, but RAFT output is estimated optical flow, not exact particle GT flow.
* **Parallel 1D landscape:** at identical E values, fixed nu, fixed scene and timing, evaluate existing WonderPlay rasterized-flow loss and existing world-coordinate `chamfer_distance(pos[None], pts_gt[None])[0]` at matching frames. Keep separate curves and record both normalization/units. No tracking IDs are needed for Chamfer. This tests whether the image-flow supervision and 3D geometry prefer different E regions, within the model mismatch caveat.
* **Not yet justified:** direct particle-index MSE between PLY and Warp particles, or a PLY-derived optical-flow target with the same mask/occlusion semantics as WonderPlay. The Gaussian-to-PLY correspondence and a visibility-aware rasterization/projection of the GT particles have not been established. GT-material injection into Warp is a model-generated trajectory, not an independent GT optical flow.

## Minimal implementation and recommended first experiment

Add an optional read-only 3D geometry scoring branch to the existing Droplet forward evaluator (`scripts/pacnerf_material_landscape.py`): after each rollout, use its already computed inverse-transformed world positions, load the manifest's `geometry_path` for each selected frame and compute the same `pytorch3d.loss.chamfer_distance` as the original trainer. Reuse the existing WonderPlay objective and initial state. Cache PLY tensors per scene process or evaluate one E per process if required for memory isolation; save geometry loss by frame, mean, failed-frame status and source hashes alongside WonderPlay loss. First run a 3-point E pilot (initial, between initial and GT, converted GT), checking frame-0 alignment and finite Chamfer, then a log-spaced E curve. Report point-count asymmetry and optionally the two directed Chamfer components. No optimizer change or GT optical-flow claim is needed.
